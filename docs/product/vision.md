# Product Vision

## Problem

Family routines are not difficult because parents lack calendars.

They are difficult because the schedule is continuously invalidated by reality:

- a child sleeps later;
- a meal is rejected;
- a nap is short;
- a parent has an appointment;
- daycare reports an unexpected schedule;
- a child gets sick;
- the family leaves the house;
- the parent simply has a difficult day.

The caregiver then becomes the runtime engine:

> What should happen next? Do I move the snack? Does the next nap move? What matters if we are 40 minutes late? Is this optional? Did my partner already do this?

That cognitive work is the product opportunity.

## Vision

NIDO becomes a **family routine operating system** that continuously converts intent + reality into a calm, explainable next action.

The user should feel:

> “I don’t need to hold the whole day in my head.”

## Product loop

```text
PLAN
 ↓
NOW
 ↓
DO
 ↓
OBSERVE
 ↓
LOG
 ↓
RECALCULATE
 ↓
NEXT
```

## Primary promise

**NIDO does the coordination; the caregiver does the caring.**

## Product wedge

Initial use case:

A family with a young child balancing:

- meals;
- naps;
- nursing/milk routines;
- development/play;
- household prep;
- caregiver needs;
- external appointments.

## Expansion

The same architecture can later support:

- multiple children;
- daycare/school rhythms;
- medications and therapies when configured appropriately;
- household responsibilities;
- caregiver shifts;
- travel;
- family logistics.

The product should not expand until the core family-day loop is excellent.
